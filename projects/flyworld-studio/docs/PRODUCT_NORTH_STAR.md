# Product north star

FlyWorld is a scientific and educational workspace for exploring a virtual Drosophila body, brain, neural circuits, and eventually controlled experiments. Its value depends on an inspectable separation between what is drawn, what is measured, what is inferred, and what is simulated.

The foundation lets a user walk, turn, stop, feed, groom, take off, land, and play an escape animation. The head can become transparent. Selecting the illustrative brain opens a closer orbit view with metadata and evidence. Four camera modes link the world, fly, and brain without losing the user's previous world camera.

Later milestones may provide source-backed anatomical assets, compact circuits from different datasets, provenance-bearing replays, and validated experiment adapters. Those features must identify their dataset, version, evidence, geometry provenance, and limitations. A button press must never be described as an emergent neural response.

Prompt 01 delivers the platform foundation only. It does not integrate MaleCNS records, claim a functioning biological brain, or authorize the next milestone.

Prompt 02, explicitly requested after that checkpoint, adds five educational system categories, generic demo neurons and relations, collision handling, and a reversible display timeline. Users can inspect layers, replay synthetic or user-authored events, and choose reduced motion. Educational categories and scripted gestures still provide no evidence of real anatomy, physiology, or functional circuit membership. The current stop point remains before real dataset integration or neural dynamics.

Prompt 03 now supersedes that historical pre-integration stop point. It adds bounded, source-locked MaleCNS structural packages, real-record inspection, abstract graph views and a separate three-body local morphology subset. Scientific selection remains unavailable, all behavior mappings remain educational, and the next boundary is before any neural dynamics or experiment backend.

Checkpoint 03A repairs and audits those existing controls before further expansion. Reliable held movement, explicit focus, collision recovery and observable motion are prerequisites for the current exploration target. Unit/build verification is complete, and all nine browser scenarios now have passing user-run evidence across the suite and final focused rerun. Human visual acceptance remains open. The next gate is that review; a further scientific or simulation milestone needs its own scope.

Prompt 04A, subsequently authorized by the user, audits anatomy independently of the remaining human control review. It verifies eight incidental local skeletons but zero of the 110 requested circuit bodies, records coordinate/source gaps, and prepares bounded authoritative recovery. It does not register geometry to the avatar or add neural dynamics. See [Checkpoint 04A](CHECKPOINT_04A_ANATOMY_AUDIT.md).

The user next approved a full searchable catalogue and bounded on-demand circuit viewer. This authorizes official recovery and runtime native anatomy beyond 04A's historical stop. The atlas indexes 165,122 Traced CNS neurons on the server, retrieves selected official coarse skeletons and individual synaptic contacts, preserves shared native coordinates and source direction/counts, and supports upstream/downstream expansion. Five requested bodies and four reference surfaces are recovered. It remains separate from the procedural avatar. A typed behavior name is not an invented functional circuit; visual training, neural dynamics and anatomical avatar registration require separate scope. Browser and human acceptance of this new feature remain the next verification gate. See [the current atlas checkpoint](CHECKPOINT_04C_CIRCUIT_ATLAS.md).
